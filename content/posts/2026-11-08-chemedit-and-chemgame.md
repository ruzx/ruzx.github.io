ChemEdit started as a plugin for Obsidian. Its drawing engine is now also available as two standalone web apps, made mainly with **education** in mind: for students learning organic chemistry, and for teachers who want free tools that need no licence, server or account.

## ChemEdit

[ChemEdit](/chemedit/) is a structure editor with a lab notebook — a good way to practise drawing structures and keeping proper lab records.

- Draw with ChemDraw-style shortcuts, then **double-click any saved structure to edit it again**.
- Files go straight into a folder on your computer as ordinary `.svg`, `.mol`, `.sdf` or `.cdxml` files. The SVGs carry the editable drawing inside them, so they preview in Explorer, paste into Word and still open for editing.
- Experiments are Markdown notes with a stoichiometry table, a reaction scheme and a lab book (steps with reagent amounts, time stamps, photos, characterisation templates).

## ChemGame

[ChemGame](/chemgame/) is a set of short learning games for organic chemistry classes, revision or self-study:

1. **Transform Link** — match starting materials, conditions and products on a board.
2. **Route Forge** — put reagent cards in the right order and run the route.
3. **Mystery Molecule** — draw the hidden molecule from clues.
4. **Group Bingo** — mark the functional groups each molecule contains.

## Install them

Both are *progressive web apps*: open them in Chrome, Edge or Safari and choose **Install** (or **Add to Home Screen** on a phone). After that they start from your app list and work without an internet connection. There is no account and nothing is uploaded. They are teaching tools, not validated software for regulated lab work.
